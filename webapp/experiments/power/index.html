Power draw

What the station's two boards draw over USB‑C: at rest, decoding, and through half an hour of ordinary operating. One station, one meter, one afternoon.

The boards drew 0.85 to 0.90 W whatever they were doing, and averaged 0.88 W over a 29-minute session of seven QSOs. Operating cost almost nothing over receiving.

How it was measured

  • The meter: a Treedix USB‑C Explorer, in line on the main board's USB‑C port, the one next to the Ethernet jack. Its energy total was cleared as the session began and read as it ended; the times are a computer's clock.
  • One cable for the whole station. The sidekick's own USB cable was unplugged, so it ran on the 5 V that the cable between the two boards carries, and the meter saw both boards. With a cable in each board the two supplies are joined through that cable, and a meter on one of them reads only part of the current.
  • The station: an M5Stack Unit PoE‑P4 and an AtomS3 Lite, both on build 1db7af0; a QMX (60–15 m, firmware 1_04_010) on the main board's other USB port and on its own supply; the sidekick joined to a home Wi‑Fi network.
  • Nothing else was talking to the station during the session but the operator's phone. What the session contained was read from the station's log afterwards.

What this does not show

  • The peak. These are steady readings and an average. The highest draw within a slot, as each decode runs, was not recorded.
  • The sidekick running its own network, as it does in the field. Here it was a client of a home network.
  • How long a phone or a power bank lasts. A supply loses some of its energy making 5 V, and that was not measured.
  • The radio. The QMX and the amplifier have their own supplies and are not in any figure here.
  • The meter's accuracy. It measures 0.16 A with a part made for up to 6 A, and its maker gives no accuracy at this level; read the figures as “about 0.9 W”, not to two decimals. One session, one meter.
