# RFC 0014: Hermes-Lite 2 support

* **Status:** Accepted 2026-11-03 (drafted 2026-10-20; redirected 2026-10-30 from a host main board on a home computer to the P4; rescoped 2026-11-04 to the HL2 backend, several channels moving to [RFC 0016](0016-decode-scheduler.md)). Decided: §8. Still open, each settled by a dated decision when its slice needs it: §7 items 8 and 10, and item 6's enclosure and I/O board.
* **Author / Lead:** Jeff Kalikstein, KB2SLO
* **Covers:** a Hermes-Lite 2 (HL2) backend on the P4, receive and transmit, over the P4's Ethernet port; what an HL2 offers as a source of channels; the safety requirements for HL2 transmit; the station at home first, then in the field for POTA.
* **Does not cover:** several channels at once, and operating and transmitting on them, which is [RFC 0016](0016-decode-scheduler.md) for every radio, the HL2 included; an amplifier, which is the station's and not this backend's; a host main board on a computer (§4); SOTA (§3.12); the QMX station, which this RFC must leave unchanged; streaming P4 audio to a home host ([RFC 0012](rejected/0012-home-decode-server.md)); PSKReporter ([RFC 0013](0013-pskreporter.md)); renaming the project ([ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md) I6).
* **Rows:** [mg9c](../ROADMAP.md) builds S1; each later slice becomes a row when it is next.
* **Prerequisites:** decode from PSRAM — shipped 2026-10-31 (§8). It also serves the QMX build. Second: the capture path in PSRAM too — moved 2026-10-31 (§8).

---

## 1. Goal

**North star (operator, 2026-10-20): operate FT8 from an iPhone anywhere in the house, asynchronously, on the home station** — HL2, N2ADR filter board, antenna. **Extended 2026-10-30: the same station in the park for POTA.** The HL2 is the station's several-band radio: up to four receivers, each a source of channels that [RFC 0016](0016-decode-scheduler.md) decodes and operates.

**Constraints:**

* **One codebase, one main board.** The P4 stays the only main board ([main_board.md](../architecture/main_board.md)); the HL2 is a second radio backend beside the QMX, and the sidekick and web app serve the phone as they do today.
* **The QMX station is unchanged.** A P4 running the QMX decodes and transmits exactly as today.
* **Offline field operation still holds** ([system.md](../architecture/system.md), constraint 1): in the park, everything runs over the sidekick's AP.
* **Each QSO on the air is a WSJT-X QSO** ([AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), **WSJT-X is the on-air standard**).
* **Transmit ships off,** behind the requirements in §3.8.

## 2. What exists

Facts are from the working tree at `b693a5f` and from sources read on 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-30, each named where it is used, re-read at `ef4e271` where §2.1 says so; §2.2's RAM rows are from 2026-10-31, after decode moved to PSRAM.

### 2.1 In our code

* **The control seam is real.** The core talks to a radio only through `radio_control_ops_t` (`components/radio_control/include/radio_control_backend.h:8`) and its capabilities; there is no `if (QMX)` in the core ([radio.md](../architecture/radio.md)). One coupling: `radio_control_tone_lead_ms()` returns 0 for any backend that has `begin_cpfsk_tx` (`radio_control.cpp:102`), which also turns off the PTT lead and Min TX delay.
* **The receive path is already I/Q** (re-read at `ef4e271`). The QMX is decoded only from its 48 kHz 24-bit stereo I/Q, through the I/Q front end into the 6 kHz pipeline ([decode.md](../architecture/decode.md), **I/Q front end**). An HL2 receiver is the same shape of stream over Ethernet. What is still single-instance in capture and decode, and one band throughout the station, is [RFC 0016](0016-decode-scheduler.md) §2.
* **Host tests.** `host_mock` compiles `autoseq.cpp` and the pure decode helpers against shims for `esp_err`, `esp_log` and `esp_timer`; a codec component tests the same way. `tools/decode_bench/compare_decoders.py` scores ft8_lib against `jt9` over recorded slots.
* **Decode cost.** ft8_lib's shipped decode takes 1,747 ms per busy slot on the P4 ([decode.md](../architecture/decode.md)), and the P4 decodes about 70% of what `jt9` does.

## 3. Design

### 3.1 Shape

```mermaid
flowchart LR
    phone["iPhone<br/>web app"] -- "home WiFi, or the<br/>sidekick's AP in the park" --> sk["sidekick"]
    sk -- "Port A (UART)" --> p4["P4 — the station<br/>autoseq · log · settings · TX<br/>HL2 backend · decode"]
    p4 -- "Ethernet cable,<br/>link-local" --> hl2["HL2 + N2ADR"]
    hl2 -- "RF" --> ant["antenna"]
```

* **No new main board.** The P4 is the station as today, with the HL2 in place of the QMX.
* **A private link:** one Ethernet cable from the P4 to the HL2, nothing else on it. The P4 does not route and the sidekick has no Ethernet, so the home LAN cannot reach the radio (§3.9).
* **Addresses are link-local.** With no DHCP server on the link the HL2 takes 169.254.MAC[15:8].MAC[7:0] (§2.5); the P4 takes a link-local address too, finds the HL2 by discovery on 169.254/16, then talks to it by unicast.
* **The radio follows the Radio setting** (row cgc4, the P4 follows the Radio setting): the QMX and the HL2 are two `radio_control_ops_t` backends in one image, chosen at boot, and a change reboots (§8). Only the chosen radio's stack is installed, the USB host or Ethernet and lwIP, so the other allocates nothing. The image with both stacks adds about 2.7 KB of statics (§2.2, measured on `2f2caab`) to today's build, inside the budget's margin, by addition, not built ([memory.md](../architecture/memory.md)).

### 3.2 One codebase: what changes in components

1. **The HL2 as a source** of I/Q channels ([RFC 0016](0016-decode-scheduler.md) §3.9): one receiver is one source, through the built I/Q front end (§3.4).
2. **The HL2 codec and backend** (§3.3), host-tested in `host_mock`.
3. **The web app** changes only where the radio does: its name, and the HL2's telemetry where the radio bar shows the QMX's. The sidekick, pairing and the API are unchanged.

Per-channel decode state, and everything about several bands at once, is RFC 0016's (S1, S6).

### 3.3 The HL2 backend

* **The codec,** `components/hl2_proto` (name provisional): discovery, frames, control registers, 24-bit receive and 16-bit transmit packing, sequence numbers, telemetry. Host-tested; the wire contract is its header plus golden frames recorded from the operator's HL2 in S1, starting with §2.5's discovery reply.
* **Our own code,** written from the protocol documents and the gateware's behaviour. Nothing is copied from Quisk (GPL) or another client; their formulas are facts we cite.
* **Ethernet on the P4:** the EMAC, the IP101 PHY and lwIP, one UDP socket to the HL2. One receiver is 381 packets/s into the P4 and four are 1,263 (§2.3); transmit adds 381 out. The EMAC's DMA buffers stay in internal RAM (§2.2).
* **Radio control:** a new `radio_control_ops_t` table and capabilities, as radio.md's **Adding a radio** describes. The tone lead becomes its own property instead of following `begin_cpfsk_tx` (`radio_control.cpp:102`): an HL2 takes symbols like a QDX but needs the PTT lead for its own T/R relay (§3.7).
* **Gateware:** the standard `hl2b5up_main`. The receive-only variants cannot transmit.

### 3.4 Receive

* **Each receiver goes through the built I/Q front end** ([decode.md](../architecture/decode.md), **I/Q front end**): one instance per channel, at the channel's frequency relative to the receiver's NCO, 6 kHz real audio out into the decode the QMX already uses. Nothing about the front end is HL2-specific.
* **Where the NCO sits** relative to the dial, so the channel is clear of the receiver's DC and the front end's offset is fixed: S1.
* **At 48 kHz,** one receiver costs what one QMX instance does, measured at 8.4% of core 0 (decode.md); the HL2's packets add to core 0's share, unmeasured until S1.

### 3.5 The HL2 as a source of several channels

How several channels are decoded, operated and transmitted on is [RFC 0016](0016-decode-scheduler.md) (§3.9–3.11, S6). What the HL2 brings to it:

* **Up to four receivers** on the standard gateware (§2.3, §2.5), each on its own band: four is this gateware's ceiling.
* **Several channels per receiver,** as on a QMX: FT8 and FT4 on one band are two front-end instances on one receiver's stream.
* **The receive filter is shared.** The N2ADR byte serves every receiver, so it is set for the highest band listened to, as Quisk does. The lower bands get looser filtering, so ADC overload is counted per slot and reported to admission as the source's reason when it rises.
* **Core 0's load grows per receiver:** its packets (§3.3) and one front end each, about 8.4% each by addition, before decode's share (RFC 0016 §3.6).

### 3.6 Decode engine: ft8_lib

* **ft8_lib only,** the firmware's decoder. `jt9` cannot run on the P4, so this direction gives up the share of decodes `jt9` finds and ft8_lib misses (about 30%, §2.1).
* **AP decoding during a QSO** (row qauk) is the in-firmware route to part of that gain, on the replies that finish a QSO.
* **Recorded HL2 slots are scored offline** with `compare_decoders.py` against `jt9`, which keeps the gap measured.
* **The encoder stays ft8_lib's,** proved bit for bit against `ft8code` by row I40's goldens before S2 transmits.

### 3.7 Transmit

* **One transmission at a time,** on the channel RFC 0016 chooses (§3.11 there). It sets the HL2's TX NCO and the N2ADR transmit filter for that channel; no receiver moves.
* **The waveform:** GFSK with BT 2.0 for FT8 and 1.0 for FT4, the first and last symbols ramped, as ft8_lib's `demo/gen_ft8.c` (`:16`, `:28`, `:49`) and WSJT-X make it. Complex baseband at 48 kHz, offset by the audio offset from a TX NCO at the dial, written ahead into the HL2's transmit buffer (register 0x17, default 20 ms). The first tone goes out at the slot start plus 0.5 s, as on the QMX ([radio.md](../architecture/radio.md), **TX timing on a QMX**).
* **PTT before RF.** PTT rides every transmit sample, so the P4 keys with silent samples for the Min TX delay before the first tone: the QMX's `TX;`-then-`TA` sequence, which gives the HL2's own T/R relay time to switch before RF.
* **The filter before every key-down.** The N2ADR transmit LPF for the band, then the receive byte again afterwards.
* **The clock** is the P4's soft clock, set from the phone as today ([time.md](../architecture/time.md)).

### 3.8 Safety requirements

1. **Transmit is off by default.** It is enabled on this hardware only after the S2 bench procedure (10 below) has passed and is recorded in [TEST_PLAN.md](../TEST_PLAN.md).
2. **SWR protection in the client.** Γ = √(P_rev / P_fwd), with forward the larger of the two readings (§2.3). Evaluated only above a minimum forward power (Quisk uses 0.05 W, `quisk_widgets.py:172`); K consecutive readings over the limit trip it; it latches transmit off until the operator resets it in the web app. Power comes from a calibration table, Quisk's `HL2FilterE3` until this unit is calibrated.
3. **A drive cap.** Register 0x09 [31:28] has 16 steps; a compile-time maximum clamps every setting.
4. **Never sent while transmit is enabled:** a PTT hang of 31, or either watchdog-disable bit. The codec refuses to encode them, host-tested.
5. **Stream loss** relies on the gateware's two layers (§2.3), and the bench proves them: pull the Ethernet cable and reset the P4 mid-transmission into a dummy load, and record how long RF takes to stop and the watchdog's period.
6. **A runaway transmitter** is bounded by row u4dw's cap (TX watchdog).
7. **A filter write that fails stops the transmission.** Whether the MCP23008 can be read back over the HL2's I2C read is unverified; S2 finds out.
8. **A private link for the radio** (§3.9).
9. *Dropped 2026-11-04 (§8): an amplifier is not this RFC's.*
10. **The bench procedure (S2):** a dummy load through an attenuator; the occupied bandwidth of a transmission against WSJT-X's; harmonics and spurs through each band's LPF; RF start and stop against the slot boundary.
11. **A transmit frequency outside the allowed band is refused,** by the rule [RFC 0016](0016-decode-scheduler.md) §3.11 sets for every backend. On the HL2 the decode's channel sets the TX NCO, so the backend checks the NCO plus the audio offset, and the signal's bandwidth, before key-down. RigPi's HackRF path has the same check (§2.6).
12. **A gap in transmit samples ends the transmission.** The P4 never fills a gap with samples that carry PTT: that would hold the transmitter keyed and defeat the gateware's first dead-man layer (§2.3), which is what RigPi's zero-IQ fill would do on an HL2 (§2.6). If the writer falls behind by more than a set time, or the HL2 reports a transmit-buffer underflow (§3.11), the transmission stops, PTT drops, and the slot is counted and marked unclean. Host-tested against a stalled writer. No browser heartbeat: transmission is autonomous by design (§1), so the station-side bound is item 6.
13. *Dropped 2026-11-04 with the second P4 (§8).*

### 3.9 Who may key the transmitter

* **The pairing token controls our API, not the radio.** Protocol 1 has no authentication, so anything that can send the HL2 a UDP packet can stop it, start it and key it, and anything that restarts it takes the stream from us, which we see as a stream loss (§2.3).
* **Hence a private link** holding only the HL2 and the P4 (§3.1). The phone's path is unchanged: through the sidekick, behind the token ([system.md](../architecture/system.md), **Who may key the transmitter**).

### 3.10 Operating the HL2

Operating several channels (a reply on the channel heard, autoseq per channel, the log's band and mode) is [RFC 0016](0016-decode-scheduler.md) §3.10. This backend supplies the radio's name to the log and the HL2's telemetry to the radio bar.

### 3.11 Losses and timing are visible

* **Sequence gaps are detected and zero-filled** with the known number of samples, so the timeline and each block's date hold, and the slot is marked unclean.
* **Per-slot counters** on the SLOT and BDRY lines: sequence gaps, samples zero-filled, ring drops, ADC overload, and the HL2's transmit-buffer underflow and overflow flag (RADDR 0x00 [15:14]); RFC 0016 §3.10 shows them per channel.
* **Blocks are dated by their audio** as today ([decode.md](../architecture/decode.md), **The time budget**). The HL2's sample clock is not UTC; samples are never counted across slots.

### 3.12 In the park

* **The same stack in AP mode** ([system.md](../architecture/system.md), constraint 1): sidekick, P4 and HL2, no internet. Activator mode ([RFC 0008](0008-activator-mode.md)) is unchanged; several bands add park-to-park contacts heard on other bands, and hunters who count again on each band (RFC 0008 §4.6).
* **Power:** the HL2 at 12 V from the bank, as the QMX is today; the P4 on USB-C. The HL2's receive current and the total are unmeasured.
* **Antenna:** replying on the band heard needs an antenna that transmits on each band listened to. A band the antenna cannot transmit on is listen-only, and the station must know which (§7).
* **POTA, not SOTA.** On a summit the weight and the cabling favour the QMX station; SOTA is not a goal.

## 4. Alternatives

* **A host main board on a home computer** (this RFC's design until 2026-10-30, on a Mac mini and then a ThinkPad T14). It runs `jt9` and four bands with room to spare, but needs a third main board (the station actions as a shared component, an OS seam for decode, one API contract for two servers, pairing on the host), and it stays home: nothing for the park.
* **A second P4 decoding more of the HL2's bands** (this RFC's design from 2026-10-30 to 2026-11-04). Worth designing only once one P4's capacity is measured; RFC 0016 §3.12 keeps it as a direction.
* **Two clients on the HL2 directly.** Not possible: Protocol 1 sends IQ to one host, and port 1025 carries none (§2.3).
* **Other software** (WSJT-X or SparkSDR with a remote front end). Not this project: it loses our autoseq, log and phone UI.
* **RigPi with the HL2** (§2.6). No HL2 client, receive-only FT8 on one band, and anything on the LAN can drive the radio; it would also hold the HL2's one IQ stream.
* **A complex FFT in place of real audio.** It replaces the front of the monitor stage, refits the SNR constant and moves the recall floors.
* **Receive-only gateware for more bands** (`cicrx`, 10 receivers). It cannot transmit; a listen-only station is not the goal.
* **`radio-utils-emu` as the conformance test.** It differs from the HL2 in receivers, telemetry, client count and the dead-man (§2.3). A smoke-test peer only.
* **SparkSDR on the same HL2 as a live reference.** Port 1025 carries no IQ. Alternate hours instead, or score recorded slots against `jt9` (§3.6).

The second P4 is dropped as a design and kept as a direction in RFC 0016 §3.12, with §3.8 item 13 and Ask item 3. The amplifier is out of scope: §3.8 item 9 and slice S7 are dropped. Slices S3–S5 (two bands on one P4, the second P4, reply on the band heard) are RFC 0016's S6 and S5; the park is S3 here (operator).
* **2026-11-03:** accepted: the direction (§3) and the slice order (§5). P4 A runs one image holding the QMX and the HL2 backends, the radio chosen at boot and a change rebooting (§3.1); P4 B is its own build, so the linker map proves §3.8 item 13. Addresses are link-local throughout (§3.1). The HL2 stays on gateware 74.2 for S1, since 74.3 changes only DHCP and address handling and has no published build (§2.5). S1 is row mg9c (operator). *P4 B superseded 2026-11-04.*
* **2026-10-31:** a second prerequisite, done now: measure whether the capture path (the ingest ring, monitor.c's FFT buffers; §2.2) can move to PSRAM, since it is most of a second band's internal RAM. **Done-when:** `decode_bench` `process_ms` with the capture path in PSRAM against `build_o2`, `MSG` identical; and the firmware with it in PSRAM on the air, BDRY `drop0` and `mon` against an internal build, and SLOT `left` unchanged (field). Then the buffers move or stay on those numbers (operator).
* **2026-10-31:** second prerequisite, measured: `decode_bench` on the five busy slots, `1ed8694-dirty`, `build_o2` against `build_o2_capture`. `MSG` identical (341 lines); median `process_ms` at t2/f2 309.5 against 309.9 (+0.13%), `total_ms` 1755.6 against 1756.0; free internal heap 514,548 against 564,704. Firmware half, the same build both ways, 20 m (14.074 MHz), 22 slots each, about 26 decodes a slot: internal against PSRAM, BDRY `mon` median 323.0 against 324.8 ms (+0.6%), `drop` 0 in every slot of both; SLOT `dec` 1,754.5 against 1,717.0 ms and `left` +504 against +571 ms, with no slot past the boundary, which is the band, not the placement. On near-empty bands earlier the same day, decode ran 2,150 to 2,300 ms: candidates that fail spend the whole LDPC budget, so a quiet band costs more than a busy one.
