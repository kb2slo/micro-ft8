# Decode: how fast it runs, and how that is measured

What the decoder costs on each main board today, the tool that measures it, and how the SNR it reports is held to WSJT-X's. How it compares with other decoders, and what was measured and not taken, are here too; what is still planned is in its rows ([kkap](../ROADMAP.md#kkap), [qauk](../ROADMAP.md#qauk), [B86](../roadmap/ideas.md#B86), [n2e7](../ROADMAP.md#n2e7), [I26](../roadmap/ideas.md#I26)), and the decode's time budget in epic [u8mc](../roadmap/several-channels.md). The decoder itself is `ft8_lib` ([ft8_lib.md](ft8_lib.md)).

## The time budget

`ft8_audio_pipeline_run()` hands an FT8 slot to decode after **83 blocks, 13.28 s** into it (`DECODE_TRIGGER_FT8`, `decode_trigger_blocks()`), and decode must finish before the next boundary at 15.0 s: a **1.72 s guard**. It was 79 blocks, 12.64 s, a 2.36 s guard, until two workers made the later start affordable; the measurements below that name 12.64 s or 2.36 s are from then. That trigger point is a design choice, not a property of FT8 (**Decoding later in the slot**, below). The clock that matters is **candidate search plus decode** (`ftx_find_candidates()` and the per-candidate LDPC). The FFT runs as audio arrives (`monitor_process()`, ~2% of a core) and is not on that clock. Each receive slot sends that clock as a Port A log the sidekick prints: `SLOT <idx> c<found> m<n> t<n> dec<ms> left<ms> y<ms> u<units>p<searches>[!] b<c0>/<c1>`. `u` is the work the slot counted, `p` the searches it ran, and `!` marks a slot the budget cut short (**The work budget**, below). The capture task does not write it to the console UART; a line per decoded message there sat inside this timer. `dec` is `esp_timer`, sampled before the send, so a clock step in the same slot does not add to it. `left` is the soft clock's time until the slot boundary when decode returns, negative if decode ran past it. It is read after this slot's clock step, so a line with `y320` reads `left` 320 ms lower than the decode's real margin. `y` is this slot's sync step in ms, 0 if none. The step is the negated median start time of the slot's decodes, when there are at least four and the median is past 0.3 s, capped at 320 ms (`decode_sync.h`). Only a clean slot steps the clock: one boundary was crossed into it, the slot before it was whole to within one block, and the ingest ring dropped nothing from the start of that slot to this decode. A clock set or step cuts a slot short, so the slot after one is not used. `b` is each core's busy percent over this decode, core 0 then core 1: 100 means that core's idle task did not run. The counter is the FreeRTOS run-time stat (`CONFIG_FREERTOS_GENERATE_RUN_TIME_STATS` in `poe_p4/sdkconfig.defaults`), clocked by `esp_timer` at 1 µs, the same counter on both cores. A line longer than the 64-character event text is sent without `y`, so truncation cannot clip `b`. These lines are Port A logs; raising `CONFIG_LOG_MAXIMUM_LEVEL` compiles in every `ESP_LOGD` and is its own starvation. The SLOT line is also the browser debug log. The capture task sends `BDRY` the same way (its fields are in the next paragraph; `g` is blocks processed after the decode reset, about 14; `lost` is samples short of one slot at 6 kHz, 90,000 for FT8; `drop` is samples the ingest ring could not hold). A second task reads USB into a 16,384-sample ring on core 0, which the capture task drains on core 1. The capture task processes each block as soon as its last sample is in the ring, sleeping one tick (10 ms) only when the ring is empty, so decode starts within about a tick of its 79th block's audio. Each block is dated by its audio: the soft clock minus the samples queued behind it (`ft8_block_slot_idx()`). So when a decode runs past the boundary, the queued tail still belongs to its own slot, and the next slot's waterfall starts on its own audio; `left` is what shows the overrun, and `g` stays near 14. Before this, the boundary was the clock when a block was processed, and blocks were paced to a 160 ms grid; with dated blocks that pacing cost about 0.27 s of guard on 15 m (mean `dec`+`left` 2.31 s before, 2.04 s after) until it was removed (2.28 s over the next 35 slots). A 15 s slot is 93.75 blocks, so where the first block falls in the slot cycles through four positions 40 ms apart, and the margin with it: 2.2–2.37 s on that run. A slot with few messages still decodes in about 2.1–2.3 s, so on a quiet band a decode can end up to about 0.1 s past the boundary; with blocks dated by their audio that costs the next slot nothing (`lost` 720 at most, `drop` 0). On 15 m (2026-10-05) a decode past the boundary then started the next waterfall about 2 s early, decode times alternated between about −0.4 s and +1.7 s, the early slot's own decode overran in turn, and syncing from both stepped the clock +320 then −320 ms on alternate slots. The ring rejects new samples when full, so after a drop the oldest queued audio is dated late by the gap; such a slot is not clean. `SKIP <idx>` or `PAUSED <idx>` is sent the same way when a slot is not decoded. When the source's audio came up short with nothing dropped, the station channel's boundary also sends `STALL <idx> gap<ms> empty<n> at<ms>`: the longest wait between reads that returned samples, if over 50 ms, the reads that returned nothing meanwhile, and when in the slot the wait ended. A stalled ingest task shows the wait with no empty reads; a source that sent nothing shows the empty reads, each up to the read's 200 ms timeout. A run's first slot sends none: the pipeline restarted inside it (a radio's restart, or Settings › **Also decode FT4** changed), so its wait is the source coming up, not a hole. With FT4 beside FT8 on 20 m two such holes came in 12 minutes (1.72 s and 0.28 s, 2026-11-07), against none in an hour of FT8 alone; the cause is not known yet. A transmitted slot is remembered by index (`tx_slot_idx`). `was_txing` clears when the tones end, about 10 ms after 12.64 s, the same moment the 79th block arrives, so the index is what the skip checks. Autoseq lines (`AUTOSEQ`) name the state (`calling`, `replying`, `report`, `roger`, `rogers`, `signoff`) and the next message (`tx1`…`tx5`).

Each slot boundary sends `BDRY <idx> g<blocks> lost<samples> drop<samples> mon<us> fe<us>`, also a Port A log. `g` is the blocks captured after the decode reset (about 10 at 83 blocks; 14 at 79), `lost` the samples the slot came up short, `drop` the samples the ingest ring dropped, `mon` the slot's `monitor_process()` time in µs, timed in place: the capture path's cost per channel ([epic u8mc](../roadmap/several-channels.md), **A channel, and its sources**), and `fe` the I/Q path's time in µs since the last boundary (**I/Q front end**, below), the 24-bit-to-float conversion included. `fe` is summed on the ingest task, which runs ahead of the capture task by the ring's depth, so one slot's figure can hold audio of the slot before or after; a mean over slots is right, a single slot's is not. The ring is 16,384 samples, 2.7 s at 6 kHz, filled by its own ingest task, on core 0 (`ft8_audio_pipeline.cpp`, where it is created, and [main_board.md](main_board.md), **Tasks**), while the capture task drains it on core 1, beside the decode task.

## The work budget

Each slot's decode is counted in **units**, and stops before it would spend more than its budget (`decode_budget_units()`, `decode_limits.h`: FT8 alone `DECODE_BUDGET_UNITS`), so where it stops depends on the waterfall alone and the host replays it exactly. An LDPC iteration is one unit (`ftx_decode_status_t.ldpc_iterations`, which our fork of `ft8_lib` reports, [ft8_lib.md](ft8_lib.md)); a search costs `DECODE_COST_SEARCH`, and a candidate tried `DECODE_COST_CANDIDATE` beside its iterations. Iterations are the cost that varies: a failed candidate runs all 25 and a decoded one stops early, which is why a quiet slot costs more than a busy one. On the two corpora a slot counts 4,939 to 7,580 units with no budget, the in-tree empty slot 6,770 (`python3 tools/decode_recall/budget.py --units`).

* **Where it stops.** The first search always runs. Before each candidate, `decode_core_afford()` reserves its worst case (`DECODE_COST_CANDIDATE` plus every iteration); with no room, the slot takes no more candidates and no more searches, and is marked cut. Candidates come from the search in score order, so a cut drops the weakest, the first search's included: on a quiet band the first search alone is what overran.
* **What it cost in recall: nothing, on these corpora.** At 6,000 units 25 of the 62 slots are cut, and both corpora decode exactly the messages they do with no budget (285/428 and 699/1,041), as they still do at 5,700 with 38 cut; 5,500 loses 1 on the busy set, 5,000 3 there and 1 in-tree (`budget.py`). The recall harness scores the firmware's budget, and `test_budget.py` holds that an ample budget changes nothing and a tight one stops the slot without changing what came before.
* **At 10,800 the capture beside the P4 decodes what 6,000 did, and what no budget does:** 827 on 12 m and 1,954 on 20 m each way, no slot cut (`capture_ab.py --budget-units`, 2026-11-07).
* **On the air it cost 2 messages in 2,781.** The HL2 captured beside the P4 (2026-11-06: an hour of 12 m, 20 minutes of 20 m) and decoded on the host with no budget and with the P4's: nothing on 12 m, where the budget cut every slot, and 2 at −6 dB on 20 m, in second searches it stopped ([the corpus's README](../../tools/decode_bench/corpus/hl2-capture-2026-11-06/README.md); [hl2.md](hl2.md), **A capture beside the P4**). An hour on 12 m had no slot past the guard; the least `left` was 298 ms.
* **The costs are the P4's.** `decode_bench` prints each slot's searches, candidates tried and iterations beside its times. On its five slots at t2/f2, `-O2`, waterfall in PSRAM (2026-11-06), decode took 159 ms plus 0.290 ms an iteration, within 9 ms on every slot, and a search 107 ms, about 370 iterations: `DECODE_COST_SEARCH`. A candidate's own cost could not be separated from its iterations, since every slot tried 256, so `DECODE_COST_CANDIDATE` is 0 and the 159 ms is part of the margin. The fit puts an empty slot at 2,119 ms, against 2,152 ms on the air (15 m, 2026-10-05), and 6,000 units at about 1.9 s of the 2.36 s guard. The wall clock is the backstop: a negative `left` on the SLOT line means the costs are wrong.
* **Two workers carry 10,800 units, 1.8 times one worker's 6,000.** The second worker (**Channels**, below) cuts the cost of a unit on `decode_bench` from 311 µs to 212 µs, its slots' search plus decode from 1,586–1,900 ms to 1,084–1,276 ms, and every two-worker slot listed what its one-worker twin did (2026-11-07, P4 rev 1.3, 360 MHz, `-O2`, PSRAM; `test_apps/decode_bench/tools/fit_units.py` over the `today`, `full` and `full3` rows and their `_2w` twins, 4,968 to 7,874 units). Its fit puts 10,800 units at 2,306 ms and fills the 79-block guard at 11,053; a slot on the corpora counts at most 7,580. The bench runs its helper on a free core; on the air it shares core 0 with USB, ingest and the front ends, and the SLOT lines of 2026-11-07, counting the hand-off and the SLOT send, read 0.221 ms a unit for FT8 alone and 0.243 beside FT4. That time went to a later start instead (**Decoding later in the slot**): each budget is now what its start leaves at the on-air rate, with about 100 ms to spare, FT8 7,300 alone and 6,500 beside FT4 (`decode_limits.h`). The first build of the later start budgeted at 0.20 ms a unit, 7,800 alone, and a 40 m slot of 7,501 units ended 64 ms before its boundary, so 7,800 would have met it. At 83 blocks the corpora and both captures decode the same at 6,000 units as at 7,800.

## Channels

The P4 receives and decodes **channels** ([epic u8mc](../roadmap/several-channels.md)): a channel is the samples of one mode at one frequency, with its own capture and decode state. The station's channel is its mode (`g_protocol`, FT8) at t2/f2, which the QMX and the HL2 feed in turn (`kRxChannel` in `poe_p4/main/main.cpp`); beside it, from the QMX only, an FT4 channel that listens (**FT4**, below). `RX_MAX_CHANNELS` (`components/protocol/include/channel_limits.h`) is how many the image holds, 2.

* **What is a channel's own.** The pipeline config lists a source's channels (`ft8_audio_channel_config_t`, `ft8_audio_pipeline.h`), each with its index, its mode (`const ProtocolConfig*`), the waterfall's subdivisions, its dial against the radio's, and whether it is the station's. Capture keeps, per channel, the monitor and its waterfall, the FFT plan, window, frame and FFT scratch, the ingest ring, the AGC and block gains, the activity batch, and where it is in its slot (`Ft8Channel`, `ft8_audio_pipeline.cpp`); decode keeps the slot's decode in progress and the list it builds (`DecodeChannel`, `decode_pipeline.cpp`), and a one-slot hand-off to the main loop. Slot length, the decode trigger, the SLOT line's `left` and each decode's time on the air all come from the channel's mode; nothing in capture or decode reads `g_protocol`. Shared by every channel: the callsign hash table. The station's channel alone ages it, steps the clock, and drives the TX gate (`decode_applied_slot_idx`, `decode_in_progress`, the `tx_slot_idx` skip), since another channel counts its own, shorter periods; its decodes alone go to autoseq. Where each buffer lives: [memory.md](memory.md), **A channel's buffers**.
* **The finished slot is one value.** At the trigger, capture hands decode an `ft8_slot_t` (`ft8_audio_pipeline.h`): the channel, its mode, its monitor, the block gains, the slot index dated by the audio, and whether the capture was clean (**The time budget**). Decode reads nothing else of capture's, and subtracts from that waterfall, which capture takes back once `decode_slot()` returns.
* **One decode, in steps** (`components/decode_pipeline/decode_core.c`): `begin` (the SNR's noise, before any subtraction), `search`, then per candidate `skip` (the retry skip and the other frequency subdivision), `afford` (the budget, **The work budget**), `decode` (LDPC, reading the waterfall and nothing else), `commit` (the payload table, unpack, the SNR, subtraction), and `next_pass`. Run in candidate order, as `decode_slot()` and `bench_run_slot()` both do, they are the decode described below. It is the only copy of that loop: the recall harness, `decode_bench` and the firmware run the same file, so the recall floors score the P4's decode. Moving the firmware and the bench onto it changed no output: the recall decoder's under six configurations (today's, one search, three, full subtraction without neighbours, AGC off, a small cap) and `decode_bench`'s host output on its five slots matched the copies they replaced byte for byte (2026-11-04). What stays the firmware's own around it: the text dedupe, the DXpedition rewrite, the fields and flags of each list entry, and the `DEC_MAX` cap on the list.
* **A decode may run ahead of the commits before it** (`decode_core.h`), so two workers can share a search and still list the serial order's output byte for byte. A decode reads only its candidate's tone bins and a subtraction lowers only the decoded signal's (**Subtraction**, below), so each attempt records how many of its search's subtractions were committed when it read the waterfall, and its commit decodes it again if a later one lowered a bin within `n_tones` of its own: the rule the retry skip uses. The skip, the budget and the payload table are still asked at commit, in candidate order, and a decode run ahead for a candidate they refuse is discarded. The recall harness's `--lookahead` runs decodes ahead as more workers would (`tools/decode_recall/test_lookahead.py`). On both corpora, with no budget and with today's (2026-11-07), one candidate ahead (two workers) lists exactly the serial output and redoes 0.0 to 0.1% of the decodes committed, with 0.5 to 0.9% run ahead and discarded; three ahead redo 0.2 to 0.3% and discard 1.5 to 2.3%; a whole search decoded at once, which still matches, redoes 43 to 60%. So committing in order costs two workers about 1% more decodes. `decode_drive_search()` (`decode_workers.c`) is the only copy of the loop, which `decode_slot()` and the bench both call: it runs the skip, the budget and every commit on the decode task, in candidate order, and while it decodes a candidate a helper thread decodes the next one not skipped as things stand. The helper is a pthread, which ESP-IDF runs as a FreeRTOS task pinned to core 0 at priority 1 ([main_board.md](main_board.md), **Tasks**), so the host runs the same code threaded: `test_lookahead.py` holds three two-worker runs per corpus and budget to the serial output, and a mix of channels, the in-tree FT8 slots interleaved with synthetic FT4 periods (`decode_recall --synth-ft4`, a file named `ft4:<path>` decoded as FT4) in one run, to each file as its mode alone, and on them about 0.1% of decodes are redone and under 1% discarded, the lookahead-1 figures. The subtraction count a decode stamps is read with acquire and published with release after each subtraction (`decode_core.c`). If the helper cannot start, decode is serial and `DECODE helper could not start` goes out.
* **Decode runs on its own task,** `ft8_decode`, pinned to core 1 at priority 3, below the capture task's 4 on the same core, so capture processes each block as it arrives while a decode runs ([main_board.md](main_board.md), **Tasks**). Each channel has two waterfalls in turn (`ChannelBuffers`, `ft8_audio_pipeline.cpp`): at the trigger capture copies its monitor, queues the slot, and fills the other waterfall from the next block, so the ring covers USB jitter and a busy core, not a decode. The monitor copy is decode's: its waterfall, its block gains and the constants decode and the SNR read; the FFT state stays capture's. A waterfall goes back to capture when `decode_slot()` returns. The SLOT line's `dec` now includes the blocks capture processes meanwhile, which take about 2% of the core (BDRY `mon`, about 325 ms a slot). If decode still has the other waterfall at the next trigger, a decode a whole slot long, that slot is not decoded and `BUSY <idx>` goes out where SKIP and PAUSED do. A slot handed to decode is marked applied by `decode_slot()` after it publishes, not at the boundary, so the TX trigger's order holds (**The time budget**); `ft8_audio_pipeline_run()` returns only once decode is done with the channel's buffers. Its second worker is the helper above.
* **One source, its channels.** A pipeline run reads one source: each `read` fills every channel's buffer with the same count, from one read of the radio, and the ingest task writes each into that channel's ring. The capture task drains every ring in turn and sleeps a tick only when all are empty. A run is built for one set of channels; `main.cpp`'s plan (`rx_plan_tick()`, on the main loop) says which, and a change of it ends the run, as a change of radio does, so the next one starts with the channels now wanted.

## FT4

The decoder runs FT4 through the same steps as FT8 (`decode_core.c`): subtraction, the retry skip and the SNR take the mode's tones and symbols from the waterfall. On the host `decode_recall --mode ft4` decodes a 7.5 s period once its 105 symbols are in, 5.04 s after the period starts, as a channel's trigger is its mode's `total_symbols`. On the P4 it runs as a channel beside FT8 from the QMX's I/Q, while Station's `rx_ft4` is on (Settings, **Also decode FT4**):

* **Where it listens.** A second front-end instance on the same I/Q, at FT8's 13,500 Hz plus FT4's dial minus FT8's (`kFt4DefaultKhz` beside `kFt8DefaultKhz` in `porta_host.cpp`, or `ft4_band<N>`): 19,500 Hz above the LO on 20 m, 13,000 Hz below it on 40 m ([radio.md](radio.md), **The QMX's receive output**). It is refused, with `FT4 not decoded: <why>` on the debug log, where its audio would leave 21.0 kHz either side of the LO or cover the LO (`qmx_iq_channel.h`, host-tested): 15 m and 10 m, where FT4 is 66 and 106 kHz from FT8. Set up afresh by every pipeline run that has it, and FT8's reset with it, so the two decimate in step and every read gives each the same count; a run of FT8 alone in between (15 m, or the setting off) would otherwise leave FT4's filters stale and the two out of step.
* **It only listens.** It touches no TX state: an FT8 transmission keys from 0.5 to 13.14 s, over both FT4 triggers, so each period it covers skips on `tx_active`; its slots never mark the TX gate, whose index counts FT8 slots; its decodes go out on Port A with `sl` 75 and FT4's own dial in `fq`, and a reply to one is refused, "replies on FT4 are not built yet" (row [grqg](../roadmap/several-channels.md#grqg)). Its Port A lines are `SLOT/FT4`, `BDRY/FT4`, `SKIP/FT4`, `BUSY/FT4` and `PAUSED/FT4`, which tools that read `SLOT <idx>` pass over.
* **Its budget** comes from `decode_budget_units()` (`decode_budget.c`, host-tested), below.

* **Recall against jt9.** `python3 tools/decode_bench/score_ft4_edge.py <20 m dir> --ours` on the 2026-11-02 QMX corpus, the FT4 channel 19.5 kHz above the LO, near the top of the window ([its README](../../tools/decode_bench/corpus/qmx-2026-11-02/README.md), **FT4 at the window's top**): of `jt9 -5`'s 980 messages in 120 periods, 342 of 472 in the first periods and 355 of 508 in the second, 71%, with 5 extras; FT8 on the same recordings finds about 48% of jt9's (2026-11-07). These periods hold about 8 FT4 stations each.
* **What it costs.** `--costs` times both modes on the same slots: an LDPC iteration costs FT4 1.02 to 1.13 times what it costs FT8 (two runs), and a search 0.24 of FT8's, the waterfall being about half the bytes. So `DECODE_COST_SEARCH_FT4` is 90 units against FT8's 370, scaled from the P4's FT8 search, not yet timed on the P4.
* **Its start and budget** (`decode_trigger_blocks()`, `decode_budget_units()`, host-tested). FT4's first period starts at 120 blocks (5.76 s) with 7,000 units, and decodes alone. Its second period, beside FT8, starts at 105 blocks (12.54 s into FT8's slot) with 2,800 units, about 650 ms on the air, so it is done before FT8 starts at 13.28 s and the two never share the cores. FT8 has 6,500 beside FT4. Until 2026-11-07's later starts both decoded from about 12.6 s and FT8 waited behind FT4: on 40 m FT8's `left` fell to 96 ms. On the 20 m corpus at these starts and budgets FT4 finds 362 of 472 in first periods and 355 of 508 in second ones. On this corpus FT4's budget barely matters: no budget finds 701, 4,000 and 3,000 find 693, 900 finds 689; the 8 lost at 4,000 came from the second search. A busy FT4 band, a contest, has stations deeper in the candidate list and is not measured, which is why FT4 gets what room there is rather than the least that served here.
* **The SNR** is FT8's constant moved by FT4's shorter symbol (`decode_snr.c`), not fitted.

## Build: the decoder runs at `-O2`

`ft8_lib` and `decode_pipeline` compile at `-O2` (`target_compile_options` in their `CMakeLists.txt`); the rest of the image stays at ESP-IDF's default `-Og`, so it remains debuggable. Decode output is byte-identical at both levels, so the change is pure speed: about **1.9× on the P4**, against 1.1–1.2× on a desktop, because in-order cores lose more to `-Og`. It was the largest single speed lever measured.

## Measured (2026-09-23/24)

`test_apps/decode_bench/` (its README has the commands) builds one C core and one configuration table into a device image and a host binary. It decodes a few corpus slots, stored in flash, under each configuration and prints the messages and the find+decode time. The P4 image uses `poe_p4/partitions.csv` and flashes the app slot only, so `nvs` and `storage` survive. Building is an agent's job; flashing and reading the console are the operator's.

Find + decode, mean over the five busiest corpus slots (27–33 `jt9` decodes each), worst slot in brackets, one core. The bench filled 80 blocks where the firmware uses 79, so these are ~1% slow; rerun before quoting better than a few percent.

| Configuration | Decodes (5 slots) | P4 `-O2` | Host `-O2` (Apple M4) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 candidates (the previous cap) | 72 | 315 ms (335) | 6.4 ms |
| freq_osr 1, 256 candidates | 83 | 1,870 ms (1,880) | 18.3 ms |
| Shipped (t2/f2, 256 candidates) | 96 | **1,747 ms** (1,790) | 16.3 ms |
| t4/f2, 256 candidates | 100 on host | allocation failed | 14.5 ms |

What these settle:

* **Host and device decode identically.** Every configuration and slot that ran, at both optimisation levels, produced exactly the host's messages, including frequency and DT. Sweeping decoder settings on the host is therefore sound for this decoder.
* **A desktop core is ~50× a P4 core on light work and ~100× on the LDPC-heavy candidate loop**. So `jt9`-class decoders do not fit on the P4 (**Against other decoders**, below).
* **The P4 bench fits the shipped t2/f2 decode inside the guard on one core**, at about 1.77 s mean on the busiest slots (worst about 1.82 s), with the waterfall and decode working sets in PSRAM. That is the search the firmware runs (t2/f2, `kRxFreqOsr` in `poe_p4/main/main.cpp`, nfft 1920), before the second subtraction pass below. Device `decode_bench` works in static buffers the size of a firmware channel's; host tools `malloc` to size so they can run any configuration ([memory.md](memory.md)).
* **PSRAM is headroom, not a slowdown.** On the same five busy slots, t2/f2 `total_ms` internal vs PSRAM (`build_o2_internal` vs `build_o2`, 2026-10-31) differed by about 3 ms (~0.2%); `MSG` lines matched each other and the host. Internal statics dropped enough that the DRAM budget was cut to 200 KB ([memory.md](memory.md)).
* **Failing candidates are the cost.** Nearly all the time added by 256 candidates is LDPC spent on candidates that never decode.
* **Large waterfalls need PSRAM.** t2/f2's 161 KB waterfall, the decode working sets and the capture path (ingest ring, FFT buffers) live in PSRAM (`DECODE_PSRAM_BSS`); the capture path costs about 0.6% there (BDRY `mon`, [memory.md](memory.md)). t4/f2's 322 KB waterfall is [n2e7](../ROADMAP.md#n2e7)'s, blocked until two bands decode on one P4 ([main_board.md](main_board.md), **P4**).

## Decoding later in the slot

Stopping at 12.64 s, the firmware's start until 2026-11-07, cuts the tail of every signal: transmitters start about 0.5 s after the boundary, plus their clock error, so a typical signal ends 13.1–13.6 s. Decodes summed over the five busiest corpus slots, by when decode starts (host, which matches the boards exactly):

| Decode starts at | 50 candidates | 256 candidates | t2/f2, 256 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12.00 s (75 blocks) | 69 | 79 | 93 |
| **12.64 s (79, today)** | **70** | **82** | **95** |
| 12.96 s (81) | 71 | 83 | 96 |
| 13.28 s (83) | 76 | 84 | 97 |

## Stopping LDPC early

Measured 2026-10-14, not taken. A trace of every LDPC call on both corpora (14,683 calls) put **98% of LDPC iterations on candidates that never reach zero errors**. Successes mostly converge in 2–6 iterations, with a tail to 24 and plateaus of up to 17 before converging, and their error counts overlap the failures' at every iteration, so no threshold separates them. Every rule that saves time costs true `jt9` messages (lost in-tree / busy; busy-set decode time against 25 iterations):

| Rule | Lost | Busy decode |
|---|---|---|
| Cap at 20 iterations | 1 / 0 | −18% |
| Stop after 10 iterations without a better guess | 1 / 1 | −26% |
| Cap at 15 | 1 / 2 | −37% |
| Stop after 7 without a better guess | 2 / 2 | −40% |
| Stop after 5 without a better guess | 5 / 3 | −50% |
| Cap at 10 | 9 / 14 | −57% |

A stall rule beats a plain cap at equal time, but none is free, and the decode fits the guard today. Something that spends the time buys it: a later start or a second channel, both of which now spend it (**Decoding later in the slot**, **FT4**). The change would be in `bp_decode()` (`vendor/ft8/ldpc.c`), a commit on `kb2slo/ft8_lib` ([ft8_lib.md](ft8_lib.md)). The QMX panadapter reports busy-band decode about twice as fast from such a stop (its v1.16.12).

## Against other decoders

Measured 2026-09-23 on one Apple M4 core against `jt9 -8 -d 3` (WSJT-X 3.0.1), on the two corpora; `tools/decode_bench/build_variants.sh OUT_DIR` rebuilds each variant from a pinned source plus one named edit and scores them.

| Decoder | Recall, 428 set | Recall, 1,041 set | Host CPU per slot | License |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| kgoba `ft8_lib`, demo defaults | 65.7% | 65.8% | ~13 ms | MIT |
| `howard0su/ft8_lib` `55ccf01` | 72.9% | 72.2% | ~90–120 ms | MIT |
| **this firmware** (**Recall against jt9**) | 66.6% | 67.1% | ~19 ms | MIT |
| `jt9 -d 3` | reference | reference | ~1,450 ms | GPL-3.0 |

**`howard0su/ft8_lib`** is kgoba's 2023-10 release plus five switchable features; with all of them off it decodes exactly what kgoba does. Leave-one-out on the 1,041 set: subtraction −3.5 points, `time_osr` 4 −1.7, OSD −1.3, three passes −0.9. **OSD is its worst value:** 1.3 points for about 3.6× the CPU and more false decodes; it is order 1 only, and each call is a Gauss-Jordan elimination with about 40 KB on the stack (`b[174][182]`, one bit per byte), against an 8 KB decode stack. **Its subtraction is undefined behaviour:** `SUB_WF_ELEM_MAG()` adds a `+ 240` offset that belongs to absolute levels, so the result is negative, and converting a negative float to `uint8_t` differs by target (arm64 wraps, the P4's gcc saturates to 0, zeroing the tone). That partial subtraction beat a full one, which is how this firmware came to subtract half (**Subtraction**). Reported upstream as [howard0su/ft8_lib#2](https://github.com/howard0su/ft8_lib/pull/2).

Why not:

* **Port WSJT-X's decoder.** CPU: `jt9` spends 0.44–1.45 s per slot on a desktop core, and a desktop core is 50–100× a P4's (**Measured**), so 22–145 s against a 2.36 s guard. Language: Fortran, with no front end in ESP-IDF's toolchains. License: a translation is a derivative of GPL-3.0 code. Reimplementing from the published description (Franke, Somerville and Taylor, "The FT4 and FT8 Communication Protocols", QEX 2020) avoids that: algorithms are not copyrightable, code is. This is the project's reading, not legal advice.
* **Take `howard0su/ft8_lib` wholesale.** It would buy OSD and FST4 we may not want, a C++-leaning build and the subtraction bug; its gains are a feature list taken one scoreable change at a time into the decoder we vendor.

## What the chips offer

* **Vector units are integer-only on both chips** (2026-09-23): the ESP-IDF assemblers accept integer vector ops and reject float ones (`esp.vmul.s16` but not `esp.vmul.f32` on the P4), and Espressif's own P4 float DSP code uses none, only scalar `fmadd.s` and the zero-overhead loop `esp.lp.setup`. So SIMD suits the candidate search over a `uint8` waterfall, not the float LDPC, which would need a fixed-point min-sum decoder.
* **No FFT or DSP accelerator** on either chip; the P4's media and crypto blocks do not help.
* **The hot paths are `float` throughout** (`log10f`, `fast_tanh`), with no hidden `double`.
* **The FFT is not on the decode clock** (**The time budget**), so a faster FFT (`esp-dsp` in place of `kiss_fft`) buys capture-time headroom, not decode time.

## Recall against jt9

`python3 tools/decode_recall/report.py` scores the firmware's decode (`test_apps/decode_bench` `bench_run_slot`: the per-block AGC as `ft8_audio_pipeline_run()` does it, then the firmware's own decode, **Channels** below) against pinned `jt9 -8 -d 3` truth from WSJT-X 3.0.1. Truth is committed under `tools/decode_recall/truth/`; CI does not run `jt9`. The match key is normalised message text. The report is recall, recall by `jt9`'s SNR, the discordant counts (missed, and extras listed for review), and how far our SNR sits from `jt9`'s (**SNR** below). Host wall time is printed and is not device time.

Two off-air corpora, both 12 kHz. The in-tree set is the 24 WAVs in `components/ft8_lib/vendor/test/wav` (428 messages). `20m_busy` is 38 slots (1,041 messages) fetched from `howard0su/ft8_lib` at `55ccf01` into a gitignored cache, not committed. A 6 kHz configuration — today's firmware — is fed a low-pass then 2:1 decimation in the harness, as the I/Q front end filters before it decimates (**I/Q front end**). A 12 kHz configuration takes the WAVs as they are.

Flags select every shipped tunable: `time_osr`, `freq_osr`, candidates, `DEC_MAX`, `min_score`, LDPC iterations, sample rate, per-block AGC, waterfall levels, and subtraction. Levels below 256 quantise each cell onto that many codes across a 32 dB window anchored at the noise floor, one value per byte. That is the fewer-bits simulation, not packed storage.

The synthetic tier encodes two messages with the vendored GFSK synthesiser, adds white noise at a known SNR in a 2500 Hz bandwidth, and scores whether that message comes back. It is an absolute curve for this decoder. It is not a capture-path test: USB audio, the front end, and whether a full slot arrived stay on the device.

**Truth is tiered, and `jt9` is not the top tier.** Scoring against another decoder caps recall at that decoder. Built: **synthetic** (we encoded it, so recall is exact and the curve absolute) and **off-air against `jt9`** (the two corpora). Not built: synthetic through a fading channel, synthetic injected over recorded band noise (which makes congestion a dial), and **consensus** across independent decoders such as `jt9` and JTDX, the only tier that can score above WSJT-X (row [B86](../roadmap/ideas.md#B86)). **A synthesis trap:** `synth_fsk()` in `tests/tx_e2e/synth.c` is plain FSK without Gaussian shaping, an easier signal than a real one, so the recall tier uses the vendored GFSK synthesiser instead.

**Comparing two configurations uses the discordant decodes,** those one found and the other missed on the same file: on 428 messages a couple of points of total recall is noise, a discordant count of 15–2 is not.

CI runs the command on today's settings and fails if either corpus drops more than a few messages under the recorded hits (`tools/decode_recall/baseline/today.txt`), if a synthetic signal at −6 dB or stronger does not decode, or if the SNR drifts (**SNR** below). The slack is there because a marginal candidate can fall either way under a different libm. On the host that wrote the baseline, the firmware settings below recalled 285/428 and 699/1041, and the synthetic signals were solid from −18 dB up.

## I/Q front end

`components/iq_frontend` turns a complex I/Q stream into the upper-sideband audio the decoder already takes: an NCO moves the channel to 0 Hz, 2:1 half-band stages decimate it, a channel low-pass runs at the output rate, and the result is shifted up into the audio band and its real part taken. It runs offline in `tools/iq_convert` (its README has the commands), and on the P4 as the QMX's only receive path (below): the same code is measured here and runs there.

* **The QMX's receive path.** The P4 decodes the QMX only from its I/Q: there is no setting, and no audio path. `qmx_audio_read_ft8_samples()` (`poe_p4/main/qmx_transport.cpp`) converts each USB read's 48 kHz stereo 24-bit frames to float, the left channel as I and the right as Q (not negated), and runs them through one front end at 13,500 Hz above the LO, no IF trim, 6 kHz out; the ring, the monitor and the decode downstream take it as they would any 6 kHz audio. These are the settings the 2026-11-02 corpus was scored with. The front end is initialised, empty, each time the UAC stream is published. The radio is put in IQ mode at each CAT link-up ([radio.md](radio.md), **The QMX's receive output**); until it answers, the stream may still be the radio's audio, which the front end decodes as nothing, so the first slot after a link-up can be empty. The radio's headphone jack keeps playing demodulated audio in IQ mode (bench, 2026-11-03).
* **Where it runs.** In the read path, so on the ingest task, which is on core 0 while decode holds core 1 ([main_board.md](main_board.md), **Tasks**). A decode therefore costs the front end nothing, and the front end costs the decode nothing but the ring.
* **Level.** Raw I/Q is far under the radio's audio: in the 2026-11-02 corpus the front end's output has a median per-slot mean |x| 23 dB (15 m), 21 dB (20 m) and 21 dB (40 m) under the audio files'. The per-block AGC (`ft8_audio_pipeline.cpp`) is capped at ×10, and audio is already under its target of 0.1 there (about 0.04 after the gain), so the cap leaves IQ about 21 dB lower at the waterfall and no gain is applied. By arithmetic, not measured on the board, the waterfall's noise floor then sits near code 86 of 255 against 128 for audio (0.5 dB per code, `monitor.c`), clear of 0. Nothing downstream is absolute: the SNR divides the AGC out and is a ratio, and band activity is relative to each column's median. The level costs no decodes: the 2026-11-02 corpus's 20 m IQ slots decode the same (26.65 to 26.75 a slot) from −20 dBFS down to the front end's raw output, and on the P4 both paths fill the 256-candidate list (`c256/256`).

* **The channel** is named by the frequency, relative to the LO, that comes out at 1,500 Hz of audio: the dial plus 1,500 Hz, so 13,500 Hz for FT8 on a QMX: its IF is 12 kHz (QMX operating manual, §5.22), and qmx-panadapter's documentation puts the LO 12 kHz below the dial. An IF trim in Hz is added to it, and the tool prints the NCO frequency it applied, since an uncorrected trim error moves every decoded frequency by that much. Which channel carries I, and Q's sign, are settings until a real recording settles them.
* **The filter** is symmetric about the decoder's band, not about 1,500 Hz: it passes 150–3,050 Hz of audio (150–2,850 Hz at a 6 kHz output, where 3 kHz is Nyquist) and stops the other sideband from 150 Hz below the band's lower edge. The low edge is the QMX's own audio passband (about 150 Hz, found live: IQ decoded 0.01 messages per slot below 300 Hz against audio's 0.34 with the edge at 200 Hz). The decoder itself searches only from 200 Hz (`f_min` in `ft8_audio_pipeline.cpp`, up to 2,900 Hz), so the 50 Hz under it is margin, not coverage. That 300 Hz is the one sharp transition, and it is carried by the channel filter at the output rate (203 taps at 12 kHz, 103 at 6 kHz), not at 48 kHz; the half-bands only keep aliases off the band, and are 15 to 19 taps. The reasoning is in `iq_frontend.c`'s opening comment. `host_test_iq_frontend` sweeps a tone every 50 Hz across ±24 kHz: the band is flat to 0.003 dB, and the worst tone landing in it from anywhere else is 81 dB down.
* **Measured against audio on the P4** (2026-11-03, QMX 1_04_010, a 40 m end-fed half-wave; the build at `3359b6f` alternating the paths every two slots, so band drift cancels): IQ decodes **at least as well as audio, about 1% better.** Paired IQ − audio per band: 20 m +0.8%, 15 m +2.6%, 17 m +0.1%, 30 m +0.6%, 40 m +2.1% (60 m decoded nothing either way); pooled **+1.29% ± 0.57%** (95% +0.2 to +2.4%), all five bands positive. A plausible cause, not shown: the audio path kept every eighth sample with no filter (row [B85](../roadmap/declined.md#B85)), where the front end filters before decimating; offline on 20 m the filtered audio path decoded 0.7% more than the unfiltered one. The first slot after a switch decodes no worse than the second. How it was run and scored, and the raw logs: [the 2026-11-03 device run](../../tools/decode_bench/corpus/qmx-2026-11-03-device/README.md).
* **The HL2's channel** (`components/hl2_proto/include/hl2_rx_frontend.h`): its receiver's NCO sits on the dial, so the channel is named 1,500 Hz, with no IF, no trim and no image to correct, and Q negated, since the HL2 sends it the other way round (bench, 2026-11-03); its own instance, in PSRAM, on the same ingest task, and BDRY's `fe` counts it. `host_test_hl2_frontend` holds the orientation: a tone 1 kHz above the dial, as the HL2 sends it, comes out 104 dB over one 1 kHz below it, and 500 Hz to 2.5 kHz is flat.
* **One instance per channel,** 7,528 bytes, caller-owned, no statics and no heap (the object has no `.bss` or `.data`); any block size in gives the same bits out. FT8 and FT4 on one band are two instances on one stream.
* **Why not a complex FFT in place of real audio:** it would replace the front of the monitor stage, refit the SNR constant and move the recall floors; making real audio keeps the decode, the corpora and the harness as they are, for any I/Q radio.
* **No I/Q balance correction.** A gain or phase error between I and Q puts an image of the mirror channel, on the far side of the LO, into ours. The host test measures it against the textbook ratio: 0.5 dB and 2° leave the image 29.5 dB down. A signal 48 kHz from the channel, past the radio's analog filter, is sampled onto the channel itself: an alias at full level, which no balance correction removes. On a QMX (1_04_010) the uncorrected image is about 32 dB, and a single channel decodes as well as from audio mode without correcting it. **Why not a blind correction estimated across the whole window:** tried at `c00fd97`, it converged to phases of 14–65° where the image implies about 4°, and on 20 m made the image worse (25 dB against 32); the imbalance differs across the window ([the 2026-11-02 corpus](../../tools/decode_bench/corpus/qmx-2026-11-02/README.md)). A correction, when two channels on one band need one, is estimated near the channel.
* **Group delay** is 9.7 ms, constant across the band (linear phase: the half-bands' 15, 15 and 19 taps at 48, 24 and 12 kHz, and the channel filter's 103 taps at 6 kHz). A block is dated by its audio, so decodes read about 10 ms later in `dt` than from the radio's own audio, under a twentieth of a symbol and well under the sync step's 0.3 s threshold. Not compensated.
* **Cost** by count, about 3.2 M multiplies per second of 48 kHz input at a 12 kHz output (0.6 M in the channel filter at the firmware's 6 kHz). Measured on a host (x86, `-O2`) at 10 ms per 15 s slot. **On the P4** (BDRY `fe`, 2026-11-03): 1.261 s of core 0 per IQ slot (8.4%) with the 150 Hz edge, over 737 slots, and 1.145 s (7.6%) with the former 200 Hz edge and its 77-tap channel filter; core 0 reads 12% busy on an IQ slot against 4% on audio. It costs the decode nothing: over five bands the slots that ran past the boundary were as many on IQ as on audio. One instance and its read buffer are 7,528 and 3,072 bytes of internal RAM (`s_iq_frontend`, `s_iq_frames`), in `.bss`.

**The synthetic I/Q arm of the recall check.** `report.py` makes each corpus WAV into 48 kHz I/Q at that FT8 channel, with the corpus's next WAV at the same level in the mirror channel, and decodes what the front end gives back. On this host (2026-11-02), with a balanced mixer, it matched 289/428 and 700/1041 against the real arm's 285 and 699, decoded no image of the mirror, and read the same SNR (median difference 0.0 dB); the same numbers with the 150 Hz low edge (2026-11-03), as the corpora hold nothing the decoder searches below 200 Hz. At 0.5 dB and 2°, 281/428 and 663/1041, with 19 and 127 phantoms: messages from the mirror slot decoding in ours. An equally busy mirror is a worst case; on 20 m a QMX's mirror of the FT8 channel is 14.0485 MHz, in the CW segment. Whether the image costs real decodes is for the first QMX recordings to settle.

## Subtraction

After each message decodes, the firmware re-encodes it and lowers those tone bins in the waterfall, then searches again. The amount removed is half the estimated SNR (the mean tone level minus the mean of the per-symbol minimum of the other tones). That minimum runs low, so a full subtraction digs a hole; on these corpora full subtraction lost a true message and half did not. The same lowering is applied at the decoded `time_sub` and the neighbouring one, which at `time_osr` 2 is the other half of each symbol, and at every `freq_sub`. A candidate within 4 Hz of one already decoded in that pass is skipped, so the other frequency subdivision of the same signal is not subtracted twice. The second pass asks for half as many candidates (`256 / (pass + 1)`). The second search skips a candidate that already failed at the same position when no subtracted signal overlaps its tone bins (`decode_retry.h`). A decode reads only its candidate's tone bins, and a subtraction lowers only the decoded signal's, so that candidate would fail again. A search that subtracts nothing is the last. Both corpora and eight pure-noise slots decode exactly the same messages. Host find+decode fell 36% on slots with no messages, 24% with 1–9, 12% with 10–19 and 8% with 20 or more. Failed candidates run every LDPC iteration and decoded ones stop early, so a slot with few messages costs more than a busy one. On the P4 (15 m, 2026-10-05), a slot with no messages took 2,152 ms with `left` positive; on 40 m (2026-10-02), before the search stopped, it took 3.27 s. A slot with one message took about 3.2 s before the retry skip. With the skip, on 15 m (2026-10-05), slots of 6–9 messages decoded in 2.1–2.3 s, and with the antenna off empty slots took 2,145–2,152 ms.

Discordant counts are true `jt9` messages gained and lost against the previous firmware (t2/f2, 256 candidates, one search). No file filled `DEC_MAX` 32. Host find+decode is per slot over both corpora, Apple M4, `-O2`. It ranks configurations; it is not device time.

## SNR

Every SNR the station shows or sends — the `R` line, the web app's decode list, the report autoseq transmits, the log's RST — is `decode_snr_message()` (`components/decode_pipeline/decode_snr.c`). It is on WSJT-X's scale: the signal against the noise in 2500 Hz, in whole dB, floored at −24 as `jt9` is, and capped at +49, the most a report can carry (WSJT-X's `ft8code` reads a sent +55 as −46). WSJT-X's method, built here from its description rather than its code:

* **Signal:** the mean power in the decoded message's tone cell, over every symbol the waterfall holds.
* **Noise:** each frequency column's mean power over the slot; at the signal, the 10th percentile of those means within ±400 Hz of its middle tone. On a busy band the columns other stations occupy sit above that percentile.
* **The AGC divided back out.** `ft8_audio_pipeline_run()` scales each 160 ms block to a fixed level before the waterfall, and passes those gains to the decode. Each cell is divided by the gain² under its analysis frame: the four half-blocks ending at that cell, weighted by the Hann window squared. Without this, the hot gain before a busy band's stations start, or while a strong one fades, inflates the noise. Frames that reach back before the slot are not measured: on the device they hold the previous slot's audio.
* **The scale:** `10·log10(signal/noise − 1) + DECODE_SNR_FT8_OFFSET_DB`, −26.1 dB, fitted on a synthetic grid that `jt9` reads within 0.2 dB of its injected SNR. It is close to the expected value: a 320 ms Hann window has a 4.7 Hz noise bandwidth, which gives −27.3 dB, and a 160 ms symbol fills only part of that window. FT4 uses FT8's constant moved by the wider bandwidth of its shorter symbol, not fitted (row [an8x](../roadmap/several-channels.md#an8x)).

Measured with `report.py` (2026-10-10): the synthetic grid reads its injected SNR within 0.8 dB from −18 to +20 dB, and exactly from −10 dB up. Against `jt9` on the messages both decoded, our SNR minus theirs is mean +0.1 dB, sd 3.3 (in-tree, 285 messages) and +1.5, sd 2.9 (busy, 699), with slopes 0.94 and 1.01. The estimate this replaced (one waterfall cell minus the slot's 25th-percentile cell) read a median 16 dB above `jt9`, with slope 0.44. CI fails if either corpus's mean error moves more than 0.5 dB from the recorded value or its sd grows more than 0.5 dB, or if the synthetic grid reads more than 1.5 dB off at any level from −18 dB up; a change to the waterfall or the method must refit the constant. On the host the SNR costs 44 µs per slot and 4.5 µs per message; it adds 2.1 KB of static RAM and a gain per block on the heap.

**From the QMX's IQ, the SNR holds.** On the [2026-11-02 corpus](../../tools/decode_bench/corpus/qmx-2026-11-02/README.md), both decoders on the same converted file, `tools/decode_bench/score_snr.py` over its three bands: our SNR minus `jt9`'s is mean −0.02 dB, sd 2.54, 87% within 3 dB on IQ at −20 dBFS (2,989 messages); −0.46, sd 1.95, 92% on IQ at its own level, as the P4 takes it (2,944); and +0.08, sd 2.72, 84% on the radio's audio (3,009). IQ is no worse on any band (15, 20 and 40 m).

Why not:

* **A median over time** instead of the mean: off-air it read about 6 dB high. Real band noise is not steady, and `jt9` averages.
* **The mean without dividing the AGC out:** strong signals compressed (+20 dB read +15 on the synthetic grid), and the off-air sd rose to 4.0 dB.
* **One noise level for the whole band** (a global percentile): sd 5.7 dB off-air. The passband and a band's occupancy are not flat.
* **The signal's own unused tones as the noise:** sd 10.5 dB. GFSK transitions and neighbouring stations leak into them.
* **Summing the neighbouring frequency sub-bins, or a baseline per tone:** no change in the result. The baseline is taken once, at the middle tone, which is 8× cheaper.

## Band activity

While a slot is received, each 160 ms block (48 ms in FT4) also yields, for the page's slot bar ([webapp.md](webapp.md), **The slot bar's activity trace**), about how many signals the column it just added to the waterfall holds, and where (`ft8_block_activity()`). Where is a mask of 96 equal cells across the column, about 28 Hz each, half an FT8 signal's width: a cell is set when any bin in it is active (`FT8_ACTIVITY_CELLS`). A bin is active at 10 dB or more above that column's median (`FT8_ACTIVITY_THRESHOLD_CODES`), and active bins with at most two inactive bins between them (`FT8_ACTIVITY_GAP_BINS`) are one signal. It reads the first time and frequency subdivision only. The AGC scales a whole block, so a level measured against the median does not move with it. Noise alone passes about 0.1% of bins. A tone 10 dB up in one bin is about −16 dB SNR in 2500 Hz, so the weakest stations the decoder finds do not show, and signals too weak to decode do.

**Why clusters, not bins.** A station's tone spreads over about four 6.25 Hz bins, more when it is strong, so a count of active bins measured how loud the band was more than how many were on it. The first build counted bins: on 20 m (2026-10-27) slots of 21–23 decodes read 85–110 in every block. On the 38 slots of the recall corpora (`tools/decode_recall`, with `jt9`'s decode count as the reference), bins read about 30 at 10 decodes and 100–130 at 20–34. Clusters read about 13 at 10 decodes and 23–30 at 20–34: close to the station count up to about 25, then flat, because signals that close merge. A gap of one bin and of three gave the same counts. It costs a pass over about 432 codes per block, in the capture task, and a 512-byte histogram on its stack: no static RAM.

**The time axis says little on a busy band.** Every FT8 station transmits for the whole 12.6 s, so after the rise at about 0.5 s the level is flat until the signals end. What it carries is how busy the band is, and when the signals start and stop.

**Dated and batched like the decode.** A block's place in its slot comes from the audio-dated clock (`ft8_block_pos()`, the same dating as `ft8_block_slot_idx()`). A level and a 12-byte mask per block go out in batches (`ft8_activity_batch_*`: 7 FT8 blocks, about a second; 12 FT4 blocks, about half a second) as one `ACTIVITY` Port A event, with the span the cells cover (`lo_hz`, `hi_hz`, from the monitor's bins). Twelve blocks is what keeps a batch in one frame and its `/api/events` row inside the sidekick's row buffer; `host_test_porta_proto` checks the largest. The sidekick's 64-row event ring takes about one row a second more in FT8. A batch also closes at a new slot, at a skipped position, and just before the decode trigger, because the decode holds the capture task for about 2 s. The blocks queued during that decode are then processed in a burst, so on the page the first ~2 s of each slot arrive about 2 s late. Not computed while we transmit (`tx_active` or `was_txing`): that audio is not the band. Host-tested by `host_test_block_activity`. The page's scale and the threshold are set from the corpora and one live session, not yet checked across bands (TEST_PLAN, Pending).

## Measuring decode yield on the air

The bench measures time. Any change claiming to decode *more* is measured on the air, this way, not by impression:

* **The metric is unique decodes per slot.** Not total rows.
* **A/B at the same time of day, never the same evening.** Band conditions move faster than any change we would make.
* **Build the comparison version in a separate worktree**, so the two arms are not one tree at two times.
* **Full chip erase before each flash.** NVS carried over has changed behaviour between arms before.
* **60 slots (15 minutes) per arm.** Report the mean and the standard deviation, not the mean alone.
* **Change one thing at a time.**

The standard deviation is what stops a false result. In one independent project on this decoder, two arms averaged 15.38 decodes per slot each, with σ 5.70 against 6.65: a suspected regression that was band fading.
